    Mission Statement

    Our mission statement is to audaciously live & lead by example. To utilize the unique giftings God has granted us to lead, influence, and encourage each other and the watching world towards God's divine will and way. We resolve to hold one another accountable, encourage growth and development, and challenge each other to be attentive and responsive to God's calling in each other's unique way. We aim to meet to embolden ourselves and each other in order to more effectively go forth as credible witnesses to embody what a man of God is.

    Opening Discussion

    In the Christian life, it's not how you start that matters — it's how you finish. It's important to understand that you don't get right and then turn to God — you come to God and let Him work through you to grow and develop you.

    Q1: How do men fall from the faith?

    • Stay disciplined and shrewd with your habit formation to maintain your relationship with the Lord.
    • Easy to be distracted once you're out in the world and completely responsible for your faith — also tragic events. Luke 9:23
    • Need to put in actionable effort.
    • Laziness = uncontrolled desire for comfort and stability. Proverbs 26:13-15

    It's endurance that separates the men from the boys. The man that finishes the race is the "teachable man." You can't go at it alone — have to have humility, acknowledge your own sin, and realize you need the Lord.

    Q2: What does spiritual endurance look like? Hebrews 12:1-2

    • A relationship with God is at the core of building an intimate relationship with Him — prayer.
    • Can feel ordinary/boring, not a flashy thing — finding God in those little moments.
    • Can't all just be random — need to be consistently and deliberately pursuing God.

    Q3: If you feel incredibly guilty about some sin/behavior/thought process in your life — does that tend to hinder your progress with God?

    • Easier to make excuses and rationalize your sin by pushing a relationship with God to the backburner.
    • "I don't wanna deal with this right now" can lead to concealment of sin / rationalizing it out of convenience.
    • Guilt is a natural emotion which is there to signal you to turn it over to God.

    Q4: How do we finish strong?

    • Accepting that we are not perfect — but continuously and consistently choosing the Lord over our flesh. Proverbs 24:16 Proverbs 20:4

    Finishing So-So

    Typically we're our own worst enemy — reference to Noble's run-in with the law.

    Q1: Four different types of finishes — anything surprise you?

    • David being in "so-so" — when it talks so much about how he's a man after God's own heart, how can we be better?
    • Samson / some of the "cut off early" guys were quite impactful despite having less time.

    Q2: What are some practical ambushes we deal with?

    • Time — being able to make time for God and spend time with family and for your health.
    • Comfort — laziness, being content with where you're at because it's comfortable; need to be able to grow.
    • Sexual immorality — being men of integrity and not being quick to give in to our worldly desires (lust).
    • Money — can affect people in positive/negative ways; can also lead to abuse of power, women, neglect of family.

    Q3: Are you absolutely sure that you'll finish strong? No.

    • Important to identify your own faults/shortcomings — being realistic with where we're at and what's in our control.
    • The certainty could be coming from a place of pride — a dangerous trap.
    • Certain seasons of life expose bigger flaws/weak spots so that you can grow and press into Him.

    Q4: How can we be super successful professionally but still keep our relationships strong with God and family?

    • Maintain the basis of a strong faith amidst professional stressors, building strong ethos rooted in family and faith. Matthew 7:24-25

    Q5: What exactly is proven character?

    • Actions speak louder than words — proven character is saying what you do and doing what you say — having integrity.
    • Character is something you can cultivate and pass down through your family — so they can have Christ live through them the same way that He's worked through and emboldened you.
    • Not genetic, but can be passed down — must be an active and deliberate process.

    Dry Shipwreck

    Psalm 20:7

    Q1: How do we get ourselves into a "shipwreck" situation?

    • Accountability, or lack thereof — need to be able to catch yourself before it gets serious.
    • Having group accountability can be helpful in having the strength to resist temptation.
    • Nail story from last week ties in here.

    Q2: Much easier to obey/trust in the Lord when it's something you already do/already good at — much harder when you have to turn away from something you want.

    • Much harder when you have to deny yourself and follow in obedience.
    • Hard to accept what you need to improve on — even harder to be better consistently.
    • How does group accountability play into this (turning away from sin)? Certain guys better with certain things, weaker with others — good to be able to pull from each other's strengths and build up each other's weaknesses. Proverbs 27:17
    • Easier to bring to the group when the guys want you to win, be successful in your faith walk.
    • Attack from the devil that your shame/dishonor/embarrassment is too great to share what you're struggling with.

    Q3: Deception never covers disobedience.

    • Sort of a downhill spiral — the sin starts connecting to each other; David first slipped up with his eyes — porn is a visual stimulus too — both lead to sinful behavior.
    • The secular world is full of little traps that can lead to a spiral — understanding this and preparing to be obedient.
    • "Web of lies," try to cover the original sin and it just continuously spirals — never ends well.
    • Recovering addicts realized that honesty was most important.

    Q4: How can we avoid being shipwrecked?

    • Disciplined — do the hard/right thing, taking personal responsibility for what you can control.
    • Not being overconfident/overlooking warnings and precautions — surround yourself with good men, reach out to the guys. "Iceberg coming, sin on the horizon" — looks small, turns into a big problem when not properly dealt with.
    • Prayer and studying the Word — ask for help from God, ask for strength/wisdom to do what's right — then implementing what God lays on your heart into action.

    Q5: How can we refuse to compromise when it comes to obedience to the Lord?

    • James — keep praying/pleading to the Lord for help. Compromise is the easier version of just doing it right.
    • Mini-shipwrecks can sometimes get you back on track — realize that your compromise is a real problem, needs to be dealt with.
    • Have a thorough understanding of the Word to be able to actually be obedient — study the Lord's will for you.

    4/12/26 · Chapter 5

    Provider, Provision, and Character

    Q1: How can we be successful providers for our families but continue to give God the glory and praise, AND protect ourselves from corruption?

    • Staying in daily prayer, constantly asking for peace — doing a daily devotional.
    • Faith and trust in God's will prevailing — path becomes clearer, nothing for me to do but trust and walk it out.
    • Jesus told Peter — "on this rock you will build my church." As man of the family, you are that rock — must lead your own church (family) — family keeps you rooted.
    • Realize your ownership comes from God — express gratitude to God in order to better demonstrate and understand who the true provider is. 1 Samuel 16:7

    Q2: How can we model godly character to the world?

    • Constant mindfulness.
    • Easy to get caught up in life — doesn't inherently "involve God," need to be intentional about making God a part of everything you do.
    • Lead by example — be kind, go to church, do your best to be God's best version of yourself — "give guy."
    • Internal first — display publicly God's work inside of us — having humility in failures and integrity.
    • Not only being a hearer of the Word but a doer of the Word — not being a "performative Christian."

    Q3: "I have to be the best, I have to be number one." What's wrong with that?

    • God is #1 — humble yourself before God. The meek will inherit the earth.
    • Asking God to help us become what He wants us to be — maximize God's potential for us.
    • Glory for glory's sake — maximize our potential to exalt God.

    Q4: How can we make sure to keep our zeal and passion in chasing after God's calling for us and serving Him intentionally? Romans 12:11

    • Giving thanks and being cognizant of all that we've been blessed with.
    • Highs and lows of life — need to give praise and stay intentional regardless of where you are — keeping Him at the center makes this easier.
    • Constant relationship — think about the relationship like a buddy, don't just call when things are rough.
    • Hard to catch yourself in a moment of pride — almost not even a thought, just an action (our human nature).

    Small Packages and Servant Leadership

    Q1: When a man is wrapped up in himself, he makes a pretty small package.

    • Only focusing on yourself — not as big of an impact as you might think; not building a community or fostering an environment of growth.
    • Not leading anyone or serving others just leaves you alone (small).
    • Giving your best without having to always be first — when you only focus inwardly, you aren't serving anyone but yourself.

    Q2: Are you a leader? What are you doing to make sure you're a good one?

    • Humble, driven, responsible, strong in leadership; humility and teachability are important to making the most educated decisions for your followers.
    • Inherent trust and belief in us (as doctors/consultants/etc.) — leaders must take that responsibility seriously to do good by others. Pursuing your endeavors honestly.
    • Confidence in what you're doing (not prideful but confident) — never a "perfect plan" but must need to act accordingly.
    • Need to be cognizant of the fact that we're all leaders.

    Q3: Matthew 20:26-28 — how does servanthood translate to leadership?

    • Every team is as strong as its weakest link — need to invest in that link to strengthen your team.
    • Jesus was the epitome of servant leadership so we should be motivated to lead as He did.
    • We're serving the people below us to strengthen the whole unit — you're making it about others, not about yourself.

    Q4: What are some things that get in the way of us being teachable?

    • Ego; thinking you know everything/got it all figured out.
    • Getting bad advice can be detrimental to feeling receptive to another perspective — can still give respect and hear them out.
    • Being lazy — lacking desire for growth and development — taking the easy way out.
    • Learned helplessness.

    "If you never take a shot, you're gonna die with a full clip."

    Q5: How can we build an atmosphere of constructiveness around us?

    • Family/team should never be scared of coming to you for advice/feedback — build a constructive culture based on accountability, not unreasonable punishment.
    • What you surround yourself with plays a big role — life is about taking some educated risks and building strong, godly relationships to strengthen you and help with the risks.
    • Carrying the "shield" of constructiveness out of the group and into the world.

    Q6: How can we make sure we're being hospitable to believers and non-believers?

    • Be who God called you to be — love, grace, a servant to others, regardless of belief; would God still show them grace and love?
    • Even if you aren't expecting anyone/anything — if you take care of your home and your soul, you'll be in the right place to be hospitable to others.
    • Once you hone in on accountability, you can grow as a believer.

    Slipping, Forgiveness, and Repentance

    Q1: How can we be strong enough to realize when we're slipping and ask God or other godly men for help?

    • Be receptive to what the Lord has to say to us.
    • Hard to know how to hear God's direction — sometimes difficult to distinguish if something's from God or just happenstance.
    • Konrad's duck-hunting story.

    Q2: "I forgot that every little action of the common day makes or unmakes character," and "I allowed pleasure to dominate me, and I ended in horrible disgrace."

    • Accountability is important — personal discipline is paramount and planning ahead/setting goals is important to staying on track.
    • God will never do two things for you — resist for you and receive for you.
    • When you're doing well, keep doing well — it's not then time to slip. Keep doing what you're doing to keep going.

    Q3: Manasseh's forgiven.

    • We're one with Christ, and therefore, I would forgive him despite his sins.
    • Genuine repentance is different from "saying sorry" and then continuously doing the wrong thing — must feel great remorse.

    Q4: What are some of the things we've had to face and repent of — and what "fruit" has come from it?

    • Overindulgence — has led to me being on a better, more serious trajectory, leading to more success, wisdom, and clarity.
    • "Catholic guilt" — constant self-examination/reflection stopped manipulating others for personal gain, allowed greater self-inspection, and demonstrated through my humility that this brings peace and reconciliation.
    • Sexual immorality — daily struggle, must humble ourselves before God and offer godly men to be forgiven and held accountable.

    Q5: How can we practically rid ourselves of our feelings of inequity, embarrassment, regret, etc. despite knowing we've been forgiven?

    • God is patient; struggling with the same specific sin is really frustrating, although we must understand that we are not necessarily meant to understand God's forgiveness but to accept it.
    • How prideful we must be to believe our sin is too great for our God.
    • Learned helplessness — just because you are forgiven doesn't mean you immediately forget your past and move forward.
    • 1 Corinthians 10:13 — only bearable temptation / key to avoid it — will only give you what we can handle.
    • Cannot control yesterday, but I can control tomorrow.

    Failure, Faith, and Taking the Shot

    Q1: "He who has never failed cannot be great — failure is the true test of greatness."

    • Learn from failure, without it you can't know where to grow.
    • During hardest times is where you get the most positive, transformative change.
    • Roosevelt quote — even if he fails, he fails while daring greatly.

    Q2: What do we do when we're stuck at a crossroad asking God for wisdom and direction and we don't hear/feel an answer?

    • Press in more, head in the direction Scripture is guiding you towards, and pray and have faith expectantly — have to cultivate the relationship.
    • Prayer and fasting can bring us closer to God to hear His voice.

    Q3: As confident, capable men — how can we avoid taking the wrong shot/opportunity, even when it appears that we can be successful?

    • Hard to know if you took the wrong shot until it's too late — however, putting yourself in a better position to take the shot will likely end better.
    • Some people try to be too aggressive — not acting with God's will/interest in mind.
    • Feels like Moses got the short end of the stick — however, that failure allowed for God to build and develop him — now he draws his strength and confidence from God rather than himself. James 1:2-5

    Q4: Does anyone have an example of a difficult situation/problem that forced you to further develop your character?

    • Foundation in God gets put on the backburner — to experience more of the world took a sort of reframing to get back on track and build stronger, more godly character.
    • Sometimes just mindlessly active, not necessarily not thinking about God but not thinking at all — a big event led to more intentional and righteous decision-making.

    Q5: How can we utilize loneliness to further cultivate our relationship with God?

    • Monks seek isolation for decades to become closer to God — eliminate distractions to be able to press in further with God.
    • Learning to trust a promise from God — become more capable of deeper faith when waiting for God to move. Proverbs 3:11-12

    Finishing Strong

    Q1: Only he who can see the invisible can do the impossible.

    • Having vision, vision for the future, having goals is important in directing behavior.
    • Must set specific, godly goals so they can proceed godly action and behavior — when you walk in alignment with God, resting in His promises, you can do all things.

    Q2: What sort of vision does it take to finish strong? (Vision ≠ certainty)

    • Resilient attitude is necessary; when things get hard, have the strength and resilience to get up, brush off the dust, and keep fighting the good fight.
    • Conviction — allows us to stay focused on ultimate goal and be okay with making sacrifices to achieve that goal.
    • Comradery — having long-term relationships in place to strengthen and keep you accountable helps build a culture of finishing strong.
    • Vision should be full of wonder — hope, benevolence, having an open mind, and rolling with the punches.

    Q3: What does it mean to win the battle in our minds? Ephesians 6:12

    Noble's picks: Romans 12:2, 2 Corinthians 10:5, Galatians 5, Romans 7, Colossians 3:2, Isaiah 26:3

    • I may have many goals, but all I want is to be in control of my mind. I wish to avoid pervasive thoughts and have a stronger sense of awareness of my own psyche and constant reflection.
    • When you face discouraging news, frustrating circumstances, or just an abrupt change in direction — take heart and have peace in the fact that God's plan will prevail.
