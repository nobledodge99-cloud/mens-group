10-18-26 — Chapter 4

Discussion Topic #1

"Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Then you will be able to test and approve what God's will is - his good, pleasing and perfect will." -Romans 12:2

  • We are in this world but we are not of this world — if we can separate ourselves from the world it will make it easier to be in alignment with God's good, pleasing, and perfect will.
  • Being in the world but not of the world sort of feels like "take a shower but don't get wet". Spending more time in Men's group/church/Godly activity will be super helpful in changing course.

Discussion Question #1

I'm sure this rut analogy resonates with all of us — what can we do, practically, to break the cycle and get out of our ruts?

  • Identify the rut. Probe and question the rut. Focus on daily obedience and faithfulness. The battle for our minds is won through daily decisions; we do not find victory when the rut is cleared and we no longer struggle with addiction/lust/mistrust/hate/etc. — it's when we make a small decision today that is in accordance with God's perfect will and way for us and against our own flesh's tendency to create these ruts.
  • Have to do some self-inspection to identify the rut, but it's also really helpful to have group accountability. When you have a group of Godly men around you, it's easier to have a higher standard.
  • Writing things down: outline what issues/ruts/problems may be and then write out solutions. Gives a mental list/map for where to go.

Discussion Question #2

How can we be more proactive in our "rut" development?

  • Getting in our Bibles and understanding what God's truths are is the foundation to good rut development. If you can train yourself to keep your homeostasis within God's parameters it will be exponentially easier to walk in truth and in victory.
  • Winning the daily battles is how we are victorious — being obedient to God and faithful on a daily basis. Making small daily decisions of rerouting is where we win.
  • Everyone's going through something — Jesus endured the weight of all of our sins so that our burden could be easy and our yoke light. If Jesus deemed me worthy of his sacrifice I owe it to myself and to Him to push through with endurance and faithfulness.

Discussion Question #1

Looking at pages 30-31 on the concept of not waging war as Christians. Whether it be choosing to not wage war against Satan, that one habit we have, or not waging the war against the dark thought that's been in our mind. What are the consequences of choosing to remain neutral? Do you eventually start lying to yourself thinking it's normal? Do you keep losing more and more of yourself? (Konrad's request)

  • Talk things out with significant others or with members of the group. Being transparent with others. Put yourself in a negative viewpoint and identify your own weaknesses and where the devil may try to strike → prepare yourself.
  • It's difficult to be able to self-identify; sometimes other people know you better than you know yourself. Important to maintain a positive and consistent self-dialogue.
  • Utilizing the prayer requests more — so that we can make our requests known before God and the group.

10-4-26 — Chapter 2

Discussion Topic #1

"He knew two truths that we also need to know: 1. The battle for your life is won or lost in your mind. 2. Your thoughts will control you, so you have to control your thoughts."

  • Thoughts control actions — things you do without even thinking. If you aren't actively patrolling, you might make decisions you're not proud of. The more intentional you are, the more confident you are in your decisions.
  • Your thoughts are your own choice — if we can try to stay positive, that should be a relatively easy first step. Let's kick ass on Mondays; make the choice to live in a positive, enthusiastic mindset. Your speech can be uplifting or negative — why not choose to be positive.

Discussion Topic #2

"Paul had not always been a thought warrior. Check out how he described himself in Romans 7:15-24." (Philippians 4:12 & 2 Corinthians 10:3-5)

  • You need to be able to identify the battle itself before you can do anything else. I know what I'm doing is wrong and I don't want to, yet I continue to do it.
  • Thoughts are controlling his actions; he knows it's wrong and yet he keeps falling into the same problems.
  • A lot easier to do the wrong thing — requires effort, intentionality, and strength to do what's right. None of us are idiots; we aren't trying to intentionally do the wrong thing all the time, we just get lax on standards and do the wrong thing when we are being absent-minded or lazy. Sometimes we just need to take a breath and orient ourselves to our situation and what's right.
  • Is it better to be born good, or overcome your evil nature through effort?

Discussion Topic #3

"Paul compared the lies we believe to those fortresses... our lies have been reinforced over and over to become bigger and stronger." (2 Cor. 10:4 → Prov. 21:22)

  • If you tell yourself something over and over again you start to believe it yourself, even how ridiculous it seems at the beginning. You need to evaluate what you are doing vs. what you should be doing — when you're consistently neglecting what you should be doing, you can get into the mindset that that's the new standard.
  • Strongholds seem invincible at first — they can be broken down with the right tools, the power from God.
  • When I think of walls: people can put themselves on a pedestal of human behavior — further lying to ourselves to justify the lies we've told ourselves. We need to be most skeptical and scrutinizing of our own strongholds and the lies we've told ourselves... not in a negative way, but self-evaluation is so important.

Discussion Topic #4

"Admitting that you need a power you don't possess is vital... It takes real strength to admit, I can't do this on my own. I need a power greater than I possess." (Eph. 1:19-20)

  • I think that realizing that you have a mental blockage is important because then you might realize that you are in need of a power greater than I possess. If you can say that, then you can be conscious of the problem and ask God for the help you need.
  • Humble ourselves before others and before God and he will empower us with what he needs. I go to other guys with my problems all the time — get clarity from the fellas and from God.
  • If we continue with our false confidence and pride — could be excused, embarrassing, or even worse, false prophets, poisoning others. Jesus cast the most judgement against hypocrites.
