Winning the War in Your Mind

Meeting notes, newest first

10-4-26 — Chapter 2

Discussion Topic #1

"He knew two truths that we also need to know: 1. The battle for your life is won or lost in your mind. 2. Your thoughts will control you, so you have to control your thoughts."

  • Thoughts control actions — things you do without even thinking. If you aren't actively patrolling, you might make decisions you're not proud of. The more intentional you are, the more confident you are in your decisions.
  • Your thoughts are your own choice — if we can try to stay positive, that should be a relatively easy first step. Let's kick ass on Mondays; make the choice to live in a positive, enthusiastic mindset. Your speech can be uplifting or negative — why not choose to be positive.

Discussion Topic #2

"Paul had not always been a thought warrior. Check out how he described himself in Romans 7:15-24." (Philippians 4:12 & 2 Corinthians 10:3-5)

  • You need to be able to identify the battle itself before you can do anything else. I know what I'm doing is wrong and I don't want to, yet I continue to do it.
  • Thoughts are controlling his actions; he knows it's wrong and yet he keeps falling into the same problems.
  • A lot easier to do the wrong thing — requires effort, intentionality, and strength to do what's right. None of us are idiots; we aren't trying to intentionally do the wrong thing all the time, we just get lax on standards and do the wrong thing when we are being absent-minded or lazy. Sometimes we just need to take a breath and orient ourselves to our situation and what's right.
  • Is it better to be born good, or overcome your evil nature through effort?

Discussion Topic #3

"Paul compared the lies we believe to those fortresses... our lies have been reinforced over and over to become bigger and stronger." (2 Cor. 10:4 → Prov. 21:22)

  • If you tell yourself something over and over again you start to believe it yourself, even how ridiculous it seems at the beginning. You need to evaluate what you are doing vs. what you should be doing — when you're consistently neglecting what you should be doing, you can get into the mindset that that's the new standard.
  • Strongholds seem invincible at first — they can be broken down with the right tools, the power from God.
  • When I think of walls: people can put themselves on a pedestal of human behavior — further lying to ourselves to justify the lies we've told ourselves. We need to be most skeptical and scrutinizing of our own strongholds and the lies we've told ourselves... not in a negative way, but self-evaluation is so important.

Discussion Topic #4

"Admitting that you need a power you don't possess is vital... It takes real strength to admit, I can't do this on my own. I need a power greater than I possess." (Eph. 1:19-20)

  • I think that realizing that you have a mental blockage is important because then you might realize that you are in need of a power greater than I possess. If you can say that, then you can be conscious of the problem and ask God for the help you need.
  • Humble ourselves before others and before God and he will empower us with what he needs. I go to other guys with my problems all the time — get clarity from the fellas and from God.
  • If we continue with our false confidence and pride — could be excused, embarrassing, or even worse, false prophets, poisoning others. Jesus cast the most judgement against hypocrites.

Prayer Requests

  • The strength/wisdom to be intentional and have endurance throughout this week
  • Getting into a strong routine — intentional relationship building
  • CVResp Exam 2 tomorrow morning
9-27-26 — Chapter 1

Discussion Topic #1

"I wonder if you feel locked up or taken captive. If so, have you considered that you might be trapped in a self-made prison?"

  • Sometimes it's easier to have negative self-talk as a man.
  • It's a really good thing that God didn't care about Paul's past and focused on his plan for Paul's future and God's calling in our own lives.
  • Having big dreams/aspirations for what I want to accomplish — when something doesn't go my way it definitely gets me down. Important to celebrate the little victories and small gains to keep moving forward and get out of a pessimistic mindset.

Discussion Topic #2

"Satan is your enemy, and every day he is prowling around (1 Peter 5:8), watching you, looking for a place where you might believe a lie."

  • Satan doesn't use completely unbelievable lies — they're based on our weaknesses/shortcomings.
  • You have to be brave in asking for help / going to seek God and peace based on your circumstances.
  • You have to be searching for the gaps/cracks in the armor where you could get tagged with a lie, and identify it so you can find the fix.

Discussion Question #1

  • I've lied to myself about how I'm living my life — I've felt invincible, or that I was super into my faith walk when I wasn't in fact living how I should be. Letting ego get in the way and cloud my judgement (something bad/unfortunate happening typically clears that up).
  • In the workplace, I have to do things other people's way and feel like I might not have good ideas — stop limiting myself by putting my own capabilities in a box and trusting myself.
  • "All believers" have some sins that they habitually commit and have attributed to "being alright" — adjusting the standards for themselves and how they view sin as a whole. Then other sins (the ones they don't struggle with) are still upheld as being awful, deplorable things, and through that lens they condemn and judge others. It's religion/faith based on convenience, not on truth.

9-30-26 — Chapter 1

Discussion Topic #1

"I wonder if you feel locked up or taken captive. If so, have you considered that you might be trapped in a self-made prison?"

  • Self-made; most of the problems we face are just from our own thoughts — whether they're actually true or not. Worried thoughts, fear, obstacles, etc. handicap my mentality.
  • You definitely can — your mind is everything. The Devil will try to distract you in whatever way is most hindering for you, especially during prayer.
  • Mind is really powerful — we view the world through the lens we have set up in our minds. There's a very real battle that exists in this world and our mind is the battlefield.

Discussion Topic #2

"Satan is your enemy, and every day he is prowling around (1 Peter 5:8), watching you, looking for a place where you might believe a lie."

  • I think your mind/the devil thwarts yourself — might make you less aggressive/passionate, creating a false self. Saying that you can't or you shouldn't do the right thing.
  • Satan doesn't have the omnipotence of God, but is an exceptional liar. The lies don't have to be big, life-changing; they're little things that compound over time to put you into a habitually bad situation. He wants to keep you from the intimate relationships of whom you love most — a relationship ruiner, making you more vulnerable in the future.
  • The devil hates you. Impactful realization. The battle needs to be treated that seriously. It's you and God vs. Satan, the world, and the flesh. Each of those three can be working against us independently and uniquely.

Discussion Question #1

What's a lie you've told yourself that was limiting to your goals or your faith walk?

  • Social media trap — lying to you about standards of living/finances/physique/etc. You play the comparison game and start feeling your standards shifting in accordance with what you're viewing. I just have to turn everything to God and have faith that His will, will be done.
  • Young men with lustful sin — a ubiquitous struggle, especially in this modern age. But there's beauty in honesty with yourself, there's beauty in recognizing your own shortcomings. Identifying these things and humbling yourself — your sin has been paid for and you are uplifted by the Lord. This only happens through self-accountability. In terms of casting judgement, we have to draw attention to coming together in brotherhood/partnership to uplift each other.
