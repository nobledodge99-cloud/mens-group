    Wild at Heart

    "Man was made in the image of God... and my God is a warrior." Exodus 15:3

    DT #1: A man's heart reflects the man... Proverbs 27:19

    • A man's heart is meant to be wild — your values/ethos/heart is the reflection of you as a man.
    • What do you do when no one's watching — how would you treat a complete stranger?
    • Almost harder to lean on God and project His love and values when times are good.

    DT #2: Christianity, as it currently is, has done damage to masculinity.

    • Your faith is meant to be projected, raw, and passionate — church has become a polite, watered-down ceremony, even prayer: "bow your head and close your eyes."
    • We need the "dangerous men" to enact change, to spread the word and love and passion of God — we need the fire, passion, excitement, and adventure in following God.
    • Problem may not even lie with "performative Christianity" but with passivity and lukewarm faith — we need to be vigilant and dangerous towards sin, attacks, and advances of the devil.
    • Good men aren't violent, but they're capable of it (Jordan Peterson quote).
    • The modern world glorifies sedentary life — distracts from what we were called to do, who we were meant to be.

    DQ: What was God's intention when making man to have something fierce in his heart?

    • The sense of responsibility to protect, provide, and lead my family — need to lead your wife and children before you lead anyone else.
    • Man yearns for peace and yet seeks war — I get satisfaction from doing really hard stuff. Men are builders, innovators, conquerors, warriors — God created man to create — the brick and mortar of earth.
    • Put God first, your wife second, your kids third, and yourself last.
    • Almost primal — Adam formed directly from the earth, at our origin we were built from dust: rugged, tough, strong. Genesis 2:4-8

    DQ: What purpose does having "a beauty to love" serve?

    • Purpose, inspiration, drive — our "why" — why we fight, do good, live to provide for our beauty/family, live as men with honor, courage, integrity.
    • It isn't as fulfilling to be successful if you have no one to share it with — beauty to love results in emptiness if you're just providing for self.
    • Motivation — helps get you back on track if you start to slip.
    • Men were designed to lead and provide — having a witness to your life gives purpose and meaning, or else you're just a lonely man fighting lonely battles.

    Staying Wild

    DQ #1: When we begin to lose touch with living wildly, that innate side of us may begin to die — how can we ensure we don't let that part of us die?

    • Important to have your own "small tribe" to fall back on and draw strength from — helps to maintain focus and diligence.
    • Keeping your mind sharp and attuned to God's will. Also being present.
    • "No man has the right to be an amateur" — Socrates; we must be good stewards of what God's blessed us with.

    DT #1: "For after years of living in a cage, a lion no longer even believes it is a lion."

    • Routine frustration; must attack and be grateful for everyday — embrace growth: spirituality, physicality, and mentally.
    • People aren't living the way they were called to by God.
    • Explore, discover, defend, and protect — translating that to our time is making out your God-given potential.
    • Getting into the mentality that Monday sets the tone for the week; can't fall into the trap of content.

    DQ #2: How can we replace anger, lust, and fear with valiance and dangerousness?

    • Being intentional about what we're putting into our minds and being thoughtful with our specific outlets.
    • Must recognize and take accountability for our shortcomings/weaknesses and, through prayer and petition, lay them at the cross.
    • Honesty is difficult — need to be specific and intentional with facing your shortcomings.
    • The negativity in the world exists to demonstrate the juxtaposition between good and evil — with God we must strive towards goodness.

    DT #2: It's no coincidence that many men fall into an affair, not for love...

    • Focusing on what God's called you to will help you to avoid the pitfalls of life; the more you have, the more you have to lose.

    Let us not pick only those battles we are sure to win, only those adventures we are sure to handle, only those beauties we are sure to rescue. Rather — let us live with passion, purpose, and intentionality, being good stewards of our God-given talents and abilities to give Him the glory and praise.

    The Question Every Man Carries

    DT #1: "Do I have what it takes? Am I powerful? Until a man knows he's a man, he will forever be trying to prove he is one, while at the same time shrinking from anything that might reveal he is not."

    • Powerful — growing into manhood, started feeling like a man when I could exert physical power.
    • When you understand that you're the decision maker and provider and everything is on you — can be daunting, sort of just have to settle into it.

    DQ #1: What are some of the most important aspects of fatherhood and mentorship?

    • Massively important in the development of young men — helps to give direction, wisdom, and purpose; should sew into others just as you've been sewn into.
    • Gotta show 'em the way — your role is to teach and lead by example, instilling confidence they can continue when I'm gone.
    • Father's consistency is important — let kids reason/respond but still teach/correct effectively (not out of random).

    DQ #2: How important is our perceived strength/dangerousness — how important is it that others view us that way?

    • "Men should be dangerous" — no virtue in self-control if you have no ability to do anything out of weakness — the ability and knowing to have the ability to be dangerous/powerful is important to how you respond.
    • Your confidence in your own capacity to enact change/exert force/authority is what's most important.
    • In some scenarios — father, mentor, leader — I feel like it's important for your sons, mentees, or subordinates to hold the perception that we are powerful/strong/dangerous.

    DT #2: "But behind me was a string of casualties — people I had hurt, or dismissed."

    • As I look back into the Word and God's will — how can I reconcile my past where I may have caused unnecessary drag to others.

    DT #3: ...two basic options — overcompensation vs. passive (retreating men): driven (violent man), passive (retreating men).

    • Obviously a mix of both — sometimes you need to retreat to regroup in order to proceed in alignment and confidence.
    • Important to have an understanding of our roots and move forward with your own definition of what being a man actually is for you.
    • Strength in retreating and being passionate — important for us to personally define the contexts in which those are employed.

    The Wound

    DT #1: Nothing worth having comes without some kind of fight.

    • Even our relationship with God — the world is constantly trying to rip us apart.
    • Money-thrown-into-the-fire story — effort gives value.
    • Anything worth something is worth competing for — the process of earning something is important.

    DQ #1: Do you believe the "Christian poster-boy" — quiet, docile, nonviolent/nonpassionate man — is what God wanted for man, is that our design?

    • God designed us to be creators, strong, passionate — not passive, weak, and docile. Men need pressure to grow and provide for their families and others.
    • Men are meant to lead by example with wisdom, humility, integrity, passion, and strength. Biggest struggle is staying connected to that dangerous/godly part of you despite the world's attempts to emasculate you.
    • Man's ability to "try to figure it out" is massively important. It's also important to be surrounded with other strong men to further encourage masculine boldness and passion.

    DQ #2: How can we, as a group, resolve to stay dangerous — spiritually, physically, and emotionally/relationally?

    • Staying dangerous is getting better every day: confidence, being bold, and staying consistent are how you stay dangerous — can't make decisions based on other people's satisfaction, should be aligned with God's will → discipline. When you get/achieve something super special, remember how you got there and who was there when you did.

    DT #2: Yes, a man is a dangerous thing. So is a scalpel.

    • Preparation and intentionality build dangerousness, so when they're called to act they can act with precision.
    • God wouldn't put you in a situation you aren't able to surmount — take heart and have faith that your strength with God is sufficient.

    DQ #3: How many times have we accepted challenges/immediate defeat based on the question of "Am I good enough?"

    • Constantly — the answer is typically yes, but it does present an almost constant challenge. Am I good enough for X, Y, Z? I definitely feel that pressure. Risks can be scary, but they also provide an opportunity to have God work through you.
    • Teddy Roosevelt quote — man in the arena.
    • Have to sharpen/heighten/train yourself constantly in order to grow and live with a bold confidence and tackle the objectives that you might not know if you can really handle.
    • Must be sharpening yourself — can't just "leave it to God," you need to work hard and God will come alongside you.

    Discussion point: Eve is a garden of delight (Song of Songs 4:16) — but she's not everything you want, everything you need — not even close.

    The Mask

    DQ #1: Can you think of times in your life where you can feel yourself wearing a mask — how does that make you feel? How can we choose to always live without the mask?

    • When you're in an unfamiliar place, sometimes you wear the mask, but you can eventually lose yourself within the fake persona.
    • How you want people to see you — "normal thing that happens" — try to change my perspective to not be so focused on how others see me.
    • Authenticity/honesty/strength can be uncomfortable — a more watered-down version could be more palatable, sometimes necessary.
    • Try to use the mask to hide your own inequities.

    DQ #2: What's the importance of trials, challenges, and adversity in our journey towards being a godly man?

    • Paramount to grow in not only your physical/mental state but also your faith. Adversity allows for a strengthening and exercising of your faith; comfort/safety can shrink our faith.
    • Moving through obstacles can give you a chip on your shoulder and confidence instilled within you to exercise your faith in the future.
    • The challenge makes the man — tiredness makes a coward of us all — seeing how you measure up to the challenges.

    DQ #3: Who here believes they acutely understand a wound they have or weakness they've identified — what do you do to counteract it?

    • Practicing gratefulness for where I am, not really thanking God for how much He's blessed you with, staying present.
    • Always "keeping the peace" can lead to a further exacerbation of the problem — sometimes you have to put your foot down and set the tone for you and your family.

    DQ #4: How many times/who can speak to the uncertainty of life — when you're just like, "God, what/why?"

    • Starting school in a new place at a DO program; fearful about a different route, coming to a new town — asking God, "where did I go wrong?" Just because your prayers were answered in a different way than you expected doesn't mean it's not for you.
    • Current stage of life — jumping right into being the leader of a young family; have to be able to make quick, tough decisions. Having a constant, intentional relationship with God is the only way to walk through difficult circumstances.

    DT #1: A man needs a much bigger orbit than a woman. He needs a mission, a life purpose, and he needs to know his name.

    • Women do not validate/grant masculinity/God's will and direction — they're massively important and we've been called to provide and protect them — but our mission and purpose come exclusively from God.

    The Father's Voice

    DT #1: The task of healing is to respect oneself... and the deepest desire of our hearts is for union with God.

    • God created us with a specific mission/calling — He intended for us to be whole and walking with Him, not walking in sin/shame/brokenness.
    • The strongest father figure/man to lead and guide you is God — we, as men, are called to be in constant relationship with Him.

    DQ #1: Is it hard for you to invite God into your daily life/challenges/circumstances? Is it hard for you to ask godly men for help?

    • Definitely something that could be improved upon — putting God at the center of everything is paramount to the overarching goal of a fulfilling, purpose-driven life.
    • "Yes to both" — work's hard and gets in the way. I try to keep it to myself and take care of things alone.
    • It's a daily decision — needs to be intentional; you won't develop a deep relationship with God or men by accident.

    DT #2: There are, in many of us, wounds so deep that only the medication of someone else to whom we may "bare our grief" can heal us.

    • Matthew 18:20 — when two or more are gathered together, I am there also.
    • Sometimes the only one who might understand is God — have to intensely press into your faith and relationship with God.
    • Sort of the intention of this group is to build godly community together.

    DT #3: God sees me... and what He sees is my sin.

    • I feel guilty when I am not adhering to what I believe when it's God's calling for me.
    • What exactly is sin? Anything coming between your relationship with God or your calling.

    DT #4: I don't trust a man who hasn't suffered; I don't let a man close to me who hasn't faced his wound.

    • As men, we're taught to bury wounds and never show our pain — that will slowly kill us all; we must tap into God's love and strength to do what's necessary. Having good men come alongside you is also so important.
    • Must face your problems/sin/circumstance — this way we can heal and grow in our faith.
    • We must help ourselves as well — not just quietly waiting for God.

    The Battle for a Man's Heart

    DT #1: Enemy-occupied territory — and if we would endeavor...

    • Sounds like Aaron Rodgers — stepping up in the pocket and trusting your receivers — not evading the conflict, standing in it, honing your strength.
    • Standing up with passion and strength for God's purpose and glory.

    DT #2: Connection between masculine warrior and masculine integrity.

    • Attack on the masculine image — the things under attack, i.e., masculine integrity, is something under attack.
    • I believe you must have the ability to enact some change — the masculine/godly integrity aspect of it is when/how do I enact that change. Doing nothing when there's nothing you are capable of doing is just weakness, not discipline.

    Three enemies: the world, the flesh, and the devil. James 3:13-18 Ephesians 2:1-3 1 John 2:15-16

    DT #3: Your flesh works against you — but your flesh is NOT you.

    Romans 7:17-25 Ezekiel 36:26 Romans 8:2-3, 9-11

    • The traitor within us revolts against our very real desire to do right and honor God.
    • The devil is working/orchestrating our flesh's desire.

    DT #4: Reframe from battling against sin to battling for strength.

    • Suppressing the strength — not ruffling feathers; sometimes you must demonstrate your strength and flex situations to being favorable to God.
    • A man must strive long and mightily with himself before he can learn fully to master himself.
    • Looking at your situations as a constantly ongoing battle forces intentionality with God to fight against and win against our sin/trespasses/temptations.

    DT #5: Why do we run from our strength/courage?

    • Accountability hurts sometimes — a lot of times we like to take the easy way out. Our relationship with God is our source of strength; must be comfortable in discomfort to grow, don't run from God.
    • Cannot run from God or run from your calling — that's the mark of a weak man; choosing to win requires sacrifice — spending something to avoid dying with nothing.

    DT #6: Where am I deriving my sense of strength?

    • The world corrupts and pollutes our sense of value — stealing away our strength/integrity/character in exchange for position and possession.
    • Must derive our moral/ethical code on God's will — our relationship with God, and Him working through us, is our strength.
    • We lose strength through looking for validation in the world — if we can feel validated through God/godly men, we don't have to compromise ourselves.
    • Let people feel the weight of who you are — and let them deal with it.

    What are some things that could lead us astray: comfortability, seeking validation, lust, fighting for position/possession, running from our strength/calling, laziness, lack of intentionality/awareness.

    8/30/26 · Chapter 9

    A Beauty to Rescue

    DT #1: Frederick Buechner and William Gurnall opening quotes.

    • Important to acknowledge your enemy is very real and present — you cannot defeat an enemy you avoid/ignore/hide from.
    • Important to come alongside others in their fight — sharing our temptations/afflictions allows us to sort of war-game defeating our enemies.

    DT #2: These men knew they were at war, yet they refused to act like it.

    • Must separate ourselves from "the traitor" within yourself — you were created for so much more.
    • Have to get out of the mindset of "that's just how I am" or "that's just always my vice" — it's us and God vs. our flesh, the world, and the devil; we are separate from our flesh.
    • Negative self-talk — assaults from the flesh/devil/world; we've all fought battles and came out on top with the Lord, and through Him we will be victorious again.
    • What am I denying/compromising on? We can't deny our own capability to grow and change in a positive way.

    DT #3: Proverbs 4:23 — guard your heart (thoughts/feelings/words).

    • Important to constantly assess the state of our heart.
    • Controlling your words/thoughts demands deliberate intentionality and constant assessment.
    • The power of life and death rests with the tongue — being cognizant; speaking life and positivity brings life and is the reflection of your heart.
    • If we are constantly engaging in negative self-talk, we may begin to bring that negativity to life.

    Joshua 1:6-7, 9; Joshua 1:5; Matthew 28:20; Hebrews 13:5

    DT #4: The Lord is a warrior, the Lord is His name.

    • The Lord is capable of anything, but He requires you to walk with Him through relationship and faith; He will be alongside you in your temptation/affliction/battles.
    • It's important to remember the Lord's might — how foolish of us to think our problems/situations too great for the Lord.
    • Modeling our lives — as best we can — after our courageous, fearless God: a blueprint to doing what's right.

    DT #5: Matthew 11:12 — the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and violent men take it by force.

    • "I'd die for them" — but would you live for them? Doing small things on a daily basis is courageous — staying in good shape, embracing lifelong learning, and leading spiritually are some marks of a disciplined spiritual warrior.
    • Staying in control and being on alert are paramount to not falling into the same pitfalls you've kept struggling with.
    • You're moving in a super positive direction, walking with God in faith and strength — one dumb night can make you feel like you haven't made any progress, but in reality the devil is attacking you because you're making such progress — keep your foot on the gas, don't forget who you are and how great your God is.
